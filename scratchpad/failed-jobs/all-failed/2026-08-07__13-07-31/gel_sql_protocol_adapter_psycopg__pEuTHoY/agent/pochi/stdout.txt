Gel speaks two wire protocols on the very same port: its native binary protocol (used by the Python `gel` client) and a PostgreSQL-compatible protocol (usable by any Postgres driver, e.g. `psycopg`). Both expose the *same* stored data, but through two very different surfaces: EdgeQL object types/links/properties on one side, and plain SQL relations on the other. The data team no longer trusts either surface on its own. Your job is to model a small music catalog in Gel, load a dataset into it over the native protocol, and then build a reconciliation tool that recomputes an agreed-upon set of figures **independently over each protocol** and reports, exactly and machine-readably, wherever the two disagree — plus any drift against a previously captured report. Everything runs locally.
